VIRALITY PREDICTION MODELING LOGIC

WHAT VIRALITY MEANS IN THIS SYSTEM

A video is labelled viral if it falls in the top 20th percentile of view
counts within its YouTube content category. A video is labelled not-viral
if it falls in the bottom 40th percentile. The middle 40% is discarded
during training: these videos are ambiguous and would inject noise. This
creates a clean binary distinction between genuinely high-performing and
genuinely low-performing content.

Critically, labelling is always within-category. A cooking video with
300K views is viral in the cooking niche. A music video with 300K views
is not viral in the music niche. Cross-category labelling would create
a biased model that simply predicts category popularity, not content quality.

DATASET

5,000 YouTube videos were collected via the YouTube Data API v3 across
10 categories: comedy, music, news, sports, cooking, gaming, education,
beauty, technology and vlogs. 500 videos per category. After labelling
and discarding the middle 40%, approximately 1,000 viral and 2,000
not-viral videos remain. The natural 1:2 ratio reflects real-world
distribution and is preserved intentionally.

MODEL ARCHITECTURE

Two LightGBM models are trained:

LGBMClassifier: Predicts viral (1) or not-viral (0). Outputs a probability.
LGBMRegressor: Predicts a continuous engagement percentile (0.0 to 1.0),
which is multiplied by 100 to produce the Virality Score displayed to users.

LightGBM was chosen for its speed, robustness to missing values, built-in
handling of class imbalance (class_weight parameter) and interpretable
feature importances that power the LLM explanation.

TRAINING PROCEDURE

Hyperparameters are tuned using Optuna with 50 trials and 5-fold
stratified cross-validation. Final evaluation on a held-out test set
(15% of data, stratified). Performance: CV ROC-AUC=0.8612,
Test ROC-AUC=0.8544, Accuracy=0.7995, Viral F1=0.6857, Not-Viral F1=0.85.

Best hyperparameters found: n_estimators=1017, max_depth=10,
learning_rate=0.0365, num_leaves=92.

FEATURE IMPORTANCES

After training, model.feature_importances_ produces a ranked list of all
20 features by their contribution to predictions. These importances are
passed to the LLM to generate specific, ranked improvement suggestions.
Features with low importance for a specific video indicate areas where
the content is already strong. High-importance features with poor scores
indicate priority areas for improvement.

INFERENCE AT UPLOAD TIME

The 12 visual and audio features are always extracted from the video file.
The 8 metadata features are filled in from user input (planned title,
post hour, post day, tag count) or set to dataset means if not provided.
Only the first 60 seconds of the video are analysed for efficiency.
