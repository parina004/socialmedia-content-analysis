WHAT IS A DEEPFAKE AND AI-GENERATED MEDIA

Synthetic media refers to any video, image, or audio that has been created or
manipulated using artificial intelligence. There are two distinct categories
that this system detects: deepfakes and AI-generated media.

A deepfake is a video in which a real person's face has been replaced or
manipulated using deep learning techniques. The word "deepfake" combines
"deep learning" and "fake." The manipulation is performed on an existing real
video: a source face is swapped onto a target body, or facial expressions
and lip movements are altered to make someone appear to say or do something
they never did. Deepfakes are identity-based attacks: they require a real
person as their target.

AI-generated media, by contrast, is content that was created entirely from
scratch by a generative AI system. No real person's face was stolen or
manipulated. Instead, a model such as Stable Diffusion, RunwayML, CogVideoX,
or VideoPoet generated the entire video or image from a text prompt or seed
image. These videos may look photorealistic but contain no real individual.

WHY THE DISTINCTION MATTERS

Deepfakes and AI-generated media leave different forensic traces.
Deepfakes often show spatial inconsistencies around the face boundary,
flickering landmarks across frames and blending artifacts where the
replaced face meets the original neck or hair. AI-generated videos
show different patterns: global frequency signatures from diffusion
model upsampling, characteristic noise textures across the entire frame,
and spatial coherence inconsistencies that affect the whole image rather
than just the face region.

A single classifier treating both as identical "fake" would miss these
structural differences. This system uses a cascaded two-stage pipeline
specifically designed to handle both types correctly.

THE CASCADE LOGIC

The system first asks: is this AI-generated? If yes, it stops there and
labels the content as AI-Generated with a confidence score. Only if the
content is not AI-generated does the system proceed to ask: is this a
deepfake or a real video? This ordering is intentional. AI-generated
content is the harder and more novel problem. Running it first prevents
the deepfake detector from being confused by content patterns it was
not designed to handle.

This system was trained on FaceForensics++, DFDC, Celeb-DF v2 for
deepfake detection and GenVideo (including CogVideoX5B, RunwayML,
StableDiffusion, VideoPoet, AnimateDiff) for AI-generated video detection.
