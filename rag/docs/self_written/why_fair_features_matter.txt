WHY FAIR FEATURES MATTER IN VIRALITY PREDICTION

THE CORE PRINCIPLE

This system is built on a single design principle: virality prediction
should measure the quality and packaging of the content itself, not the
size or history of the channel that uploaded it. Every feature was
deliberately evaluated against this standard. Some were included.
Some were excluded. The distinction is not arbitrary.

WHAT WAS DELIBERATELY EXCLUDED

Raw subscriber count was excluded because large channels receive more
views simply from exposure: their existing audience watches regardless
of content quality. A model trained with subscriber count would learn
"big channel = viral," which is tautological and useless for a new creator.

Raw view count was excluded because it is the label. Using it as a
feature would leak the answer into the training data.

Raw like count and raw comment count were excluded because they scale
with audience size. A channel with 5 million subscribers will collect
more likes than a channel with 500 subscribers even if the content from
the smaller channel is objectively better.

Channel age and total video count were excluded because they describe
creator experience and longevity, not the quality of the specific video
being uploaded.

WHY NORMALISED RATIOS ARE KEPT

Like-to-view ratio (likes divided by views) and comment-to-view ratio
(comments divided by views) are retained because dividing by view count
removes the audience size bias. A video that converts 20% of viewers
into likes is genuinely more engaging than one that converts 5%,
regardless of the channel size. These ratios measure content resonance,
not creator fame.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY MEASURED

The 20 retained features describe:
- How the video looks and sounds (12 visual and audio features)
- How the creator chose to present and package it (8 metadata features)

These are within the control of any creator at any stage of their career.
A new creator with zero subscribers can optimise every single one of these
features. An established creator cannot inflate these scores simply by
having a large audience.

WHY THIS MATTERS FOR THE SYSTEM

A fair model is a useful model. If the system told a creator "your content
has low viral potential" because they have few subscribers, the advice
would be useless and discouraging. If it tells them "your audio energy
is low, your title lacks emotional framing and you are posting on a
low-traffic day," those are specific, actionable improvements that any
creator can implement before their next upload.

This design philosophy, content quality over creator fame, is what
makes this virality predictor genuinely useful rather than a channel size
estimator in disguise.
