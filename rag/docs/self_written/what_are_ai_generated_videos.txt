WHAT ARE AI-GENERATED VIDEOS

DEFINITION

An AI-generated video is a sequence of frames created entirely by a
generative AI model, typically from a text prompt (Text-to-Video, T2V)
or from a reference image (Image-to-Video, I2V). No camera recorded
the scene. Every frame was synthesised by a neural network.

HOW TEXT-TO-VIDEO WORKS

Modern T2V systems extend image diffusion models to the temporal
dimension. The model learns not just to generate coherent single frames
but to generate sequences of frames with consistent motion, lighting,
and object persistence across time. Architecturally, this is achieved
by inserting temporal attention layers into the U-Net or transformer
backbone of an image diffusion model, allowing the model to attend
across frames as well as across spatial positions within a frame.

At inference, the model takes a text prompt and optionally a number of
frames, frame rate and resolution. It generates all frames jointly
(in the case of full video diffusion) or autoregressively (generating
frames in sequence conditioned on previous frames). Motion is
synthesised to be semantically consistent with the prompt: "a person
walking on a beach" will have coordinated leg and arm movement: but
the underlying physics and temporal coherence are approximations.

MAJOR T2V AND I2V MODELS

Sora (OpenAI): Large-scale diffusion transformer trained on video data.
Generates up to 60 seconds of high-resolution consistent video.
Introduced in 2024, not yet publicly available.

RunwayML Gen-2 and Gen-3: Commercially available T2V and I2V model.
Used in professional film and content creation. Supports motion brush
and camera control.

CogVideoX (Zhipu AI): Open-source T2V model, one of the datasets used
to train this system's AI-generated video detection capability.

VideoPoet (Google): Multimodal model handling T2V, I2V, video editing,
and video-to-audio in a unified framework.

AnimateDiff / BDAnimateDiffLightning: Plug-in for Stable Diffusion image
models that adds temporal consistency for short video clips. Widely used
for anime-style and stylised video generation.

StableDiffusion Video (Stability AI): Extends Stable Diffusion to video
via a latent diffusion approach with temporal convolutions.

WHY AI-GENERATED VIDEOS ARE HARDER TO DETECT

Static image detectors fail on video because each individual frame may
look realistic when examined alone. The forensic signal in AI-generated
video is partly temporal: subtle frame-to-frame inconsistencies in noise
patterns, unphysical motion trajectories, temporal flickering in fine
texture regions (hair, fabric, water) and inconsistent lighting
direction across frames. This system extracts 1 frame per second and
analyses frequency artifacts in each frame independently, then averages
across frames, which is effective for detecting the per-frame generation
signatures that persist throughout AI-generated video sequences.

TRAINING DATA FOR AI VIDEO DETECTION

This system was trained on the GenVideo subset of the AIGVDet dataset,
which includes AI-generated videos from CogVideoX5B, RunwayML,
StableDiffusion Video, VideoPoet, AnimateDiffLightning and DeepAction,
alongside real videos sourced from Pexels. The dataset covers both
T2V and I2V generation pipelines across multiple resolutions and styles.
