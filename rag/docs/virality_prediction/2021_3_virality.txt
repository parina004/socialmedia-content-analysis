Paper Title: Reemergence Modeling of Intelligent Information Diffusion in Heterogeneous Social Networks: The Dynamics Perspective
Authors: Yinxue Yi, Zufan Zhang, Laurence T. Yang, Chenquan Gan, Xianjun Deng, and Lingzhi Yi
Year: 2021

Problem Addressed:
Traditional diffusion models fail to account for the "reinfection-reemergence" phenomenon, where individuals participate in information spreading multiple times or hesitate due to self-confirming behavior (judging validity).
Methodology:
The authors proposed the SUIRS model (Susceptible-Uncertain-Infected-Recovered-Susceptible), which introduces a new "Uncertain" psychological state to reflect the time individuals spend judging the validity of information before forwarding it.
Key Findings:
Information diffusion depends on a specific outbreak threshold ($R_0$); if the threshold is met, the information "reemerges" and continues to spread. Social tie strength significantly impacts the velocity and range of this spread.
Limitations:
The model is highly mathematical and theoretical; while it predicts if something will spread, it does not provide the "actionable tips" for content improvement that your system offers.
