Paper Title: Structural Hole Theory in Social Network Analysis: A Review 
Authors: Zihang Lin, Yuwei Zhang, Qingyuan Gong, Yang Chen, Atte Oksanen, and Aaron Yi Ding 
Year: 2022 

1. Problem Addressed: 
The paper reviews how certain individuals, called Structural Hole (SH) spanners, occupy bridging positions between unconnected communities in a social network and the advantages they gain from this position.
2. Methodology: 
It categorizes detection algorithms for SH spanners into information flow-based (nodes that control the most paths) and network centrality-based (nodes in advantageous positions). It reviews metrics like effective size, efficiency, constraint, and hierarchy.
3. Key Findings: 
SH spanners are crucial for information diffusion. In Twitter, just 1% of users occupying SH positions control nearly 80% of information spread between different communities. However, in emotion contagion, SH spanners can be less influential than ordinary users.
4. Limitations: 
Most algorithms are designed for static networks and struggle with the high computational complexity required for large-scale, real-time social graphs.
